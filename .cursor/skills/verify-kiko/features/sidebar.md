# Sidebar

Every screen a user opens of Kiko carries a conversation column on the left: a 60px icon rail down its outer edge, a 280px panel beside it, and the transcript filling whatever is left. The panel lists the conversations a user has — pinned ones as a reflowing tile grid on top, the rest as rows beneath — and every row carries the conversation's avatar, name, latest message and unread count. The user can switch the panel's subject from the rail, hide the panel entirely and bring it back, drag its edge to a width between 200 and 420, hover any conversation for a preview of its last three messages, and right-click it to pin it or clear its unread mark. Nothing in the sidebar moves on its own: collapse and resize are instant, and the only animation anywhere in it is the typing wave.

## Sub-features

- `rail-tabs` — five rail buttons, `chats`, `terminal`, `code`, `browser` and `settings`. One is selected at a time and carries `data-active`; each carries its own `data-panel` value and its glyph is the tab's `<svg>`.
- `rail-select-and-toggle` — clicking a **different** tab switches the panel and expands it; clicking the **already-selected** tab collapses it instead of re-selecting. That one gesture is both the switcher and the second way to hide the panel.
- `panel-empty` — `terminal`, `code`, `browser` and `settings` have no content in this app yet and render one line, `the <panel> panel is not built`. Three of the five rail panels take this path in a default run.
- `panel-collapse` — the panel collapses to width `0` and carries `data-collapsed`. Three user paths reach it: the `toggle sidebar` row in the `+` menu, a click on the selected rail tab, and a resize drag past the minimum. A drag to the right from the collapsed edge brings it back, because the resize handle stays mounted at the rail's edge.
- `panel-resize` — dragging the 3px handle sets the panel width, clamped to `200`–`420`. Double-clicking the handle resets it to the default. `data-resizing` is set for the duration of the gesture only.
- `panel-resize-collapse` — dragging **below** 200 does not stop at a floor; it collapses the panel. The clamp is a ceiling, not a floor.
- `pinned-grid` — pinned conversations render as circular tiles above the rows, in a `repeat(auto-fill, minmax(64px,1fr))` grid, so the grid reflows as the panel is resized: four columns at the 280px default, five at 360px.
- `chat-rows` — every unpinned conversation is a row on a `28px minmax(0,1fr) auto` grid: a 22px avatar, then the name over the preview, then the trailing column. The selected row carries `data-active`; an unread one carries `data-unread`.
- `unread-marker` — an unread row carries `data-unread` and shows its unread number in the trailing column as the sidebar's unread count. The trailing column holds **only** that number.
- `typing-line` — a conversation whose presence is `typing` replaces its preview line with the word `typing` followed by three dots, **in the preview slot**, not on the row's right-hand edge. The preview line carries `data-typing` and is coloured with `--accent`.
- `hover-preview` — hovering either a row or a pinned tile opens a preview card to the right of the panel, showing the avatar, name, presence, persona and the conversation's last three messages.
- `row-context-menu` — right-clicking a row opens a menu at the pointer with two items: `pin`/`unpin` and `mark as read`/`mark as unread`. Both items mutate real state; pinning a conversation moves it into the pinned grid.
- `no-borders` — computed border widths across every descendant of the panel are all `0px`. There is no border anywhere in the sidebar.
- `no-motion` — collapse, resize and panel switching are instant; `transition-duration` computes to `0s` on every sidebar element. The typing dots are the only animation.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- Open `http://127.0.0.1:3001/`. The sidebar is mounted in the composed app only; there is no separate harness page and no route that reaches it.
- Click any rail tab to switch the panel's subject. Clicking the selected tab hides the panel.
- Open the `+` in the panel header and choose `toggle sidebar` to hide or show the panel.
- Drag the 3px handle on the panel's right edge to resize; drag it past the panel's left edge to collapse; double-click it to reset to the default width.
- Hover any conversation row or any pinned tile to open its preview card.
- Right-click any conversation row to pin or unpin it, or to mark it read or unread.

## Gotchas

- **`.sidebar-shell` is not `.chat-shell`, and the difference matters.** `apps/web-solid/src/main.tsx` puts `sidebar-shell` on the app's flex row and keeps `data-component="chat-shell"` on the transcript pane beside it. Every rule in `sidebar.css` is scoped under `.sidebar-shell`, so a recipe that styles by `.chat-shell` is styling the pane. Read the row's class and the pane's `data-component` separately; neither is the other.
- **The app has no `.app-shell` or `.app-pane` any more.** `main.tsx` composes the sidebar and the transcript pane directly inside `sidebar-shell`, so a recipe that asserts on `.app-shell`, or that counts the shell's children expecting exactly one, is asserting on an element that no longer exists. The stable handles are `[data-component="sidebar"]` and `[data-component="chat-shell"]`.
- **The menu content element is `[data-component="menu-v2-content"]`.** `MenuV2.Context.Content` overwrites whatever `data-component` you pass it with its own, so a `data-component` handed in as a prop never reaches the DOM. Selecting the sidebar's context menu by a prop-supplied handle silently finds nothing — scope by `[data-component="menu-v2-content"]`, then read `[data-component="menu-v2-item"]` inside it.
- **Collapsing is not hiding.** The panel keeps width `0` and stays in the DOM with `data-collapsed` on it. Assert the attribute **and** the width; a run that checks only `visibility` or `display` reports a passing collapse for an element that is still laid out.
- **The collapse has two exits and only one is obvious.** The `+` menu's `toggle sidebar` row and the resize drag back out from `x ≈ 59` both restore the panel, and neither restores the *previous* width: a drag grows out of `0` by the distance travelled. Reloading the page between a collapse and its restore is the one way to guarantee you are not testing the drag.
- **Dragging below 200 collapses; it does not stop at the floor.** A recipe that drags to the left edge and asserts "the width floors at 200" will report a real collapse as a failure of its own expectation. The clamps are asymmetric by design: 420 is a ceiling, 200 is the point past which the panel collapses.
- **The resize handle is 3px wide and lives inside the panel.** A drag that leaves the handle mid-gesture only works because the implementation captures the pointer, so a recipe must issue `mouse.down`, several `mouse.move` steps **and** `mouse.up` — a single `mouse.move` to the target and back leaves `data-resizing` set and the width unchanged. Re-read the width after `mouse.up()`, and read `data-resizing` while the button is still down.
- **The pinned grid reflows; it does not filter.** Resizing from 280 to 360 changes the column count from four to five and the tile count not at all. Assert the column count at two widths rather than asserting the tile count changes with the width.
- **The typing dots are in the preview slot, not the trailing column.** A row whose bot is typing shows the word `typing` plus three dots exactly where the preview line was; the trailing column still carries only the unread count. A recipe that queries the row's last child for the typing state finds nothing and concludes the feature is missing.
- **Only the dots move.** Across the panel and the rail — 32 elements checked — `transition-duration` is `0s` everywhere and `animation-name` is `none` everywhere except on **three** elements, the `.typing-dot` spans of the typing row, whose `animation-name` is `sidebar-typing-dot` with staggered delays. Any easing read on the panel, a row or a tile is a defect, and a no-animation probe must log how many elements it checked next to the violation count.
- **Zero borders is a computed claim, not a visual one.** Every `border-*-width` across the panel and the rail computes to `0px` — 32 elements checked, 0 violations — so do not infer it from a screenshot, and do not report a count of zero violations over a selector that matched nothing.
- **The sidebar is mounted in the composed app only.** There is no sidebar on `/src/chat/harness.html`, so a recipe run there finds zero of every handle and looks like a broken sidebar. Drive it at `/`.
- **The hover card opens on a delay in both directions.** 400ms to open, 150ms to close. A probe that reads the card synchronously after `hover()` will prove it absent; a card left on screen after the pointer leaves is the close delay, not a leak. Check the count, then check it again after the window.
- **The hover card must stay non-interactive.** `HoverCardV2` defaults `ignoreSafeArea` to `true` and `hover-card-v2.css` sets `pointer-events: none !important` on the content — `!important` because Kobalte writes `pointer-events: auto` into that element's inline style, so an ordinary declaration loses. A preview that can trap the cursor is a preview that stays on screen after the pointer has left the row it describes. So a recipe must assert **exactly one** `[data-component="hover-card-v2-content"]` even after hovering two rows in a row, must not try to click or read a link inside the card, and must judge interactivity from the **computed** style rather than the inline one.
- **Both menu items mutate the store, and the harness cannot show either.** `markRead`, `markUnread`, `togglePinned` and `messagesFor` are real store mutations, so a run that proves them from a fixture harness proves nothing — there is no sidebar there. Drive them at `/` and prove the effect in the DOM: the row's `data-unread` and unread count, and the tile/row split for a pin.
- **`bun test` resolves `solid-js` to its server build, where `createMemo` never re-runs.** Anything derived belongs in these browser recipes rather than in a unit test.
- **The four fixtures are fixed, so "the default render" is a real assertion.** Two pinned tiles, four rows, `grace` and `alan` unread, `alan` typing. A count that differs means state leaked from an earlier step in the run — reload before the fixture recipe rather than reasoning about the difference.
