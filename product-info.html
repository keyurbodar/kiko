FIELD GUIDE / A GENERAL PURPOSE AI CHAT THAT FEELS LIKE TELEGRAM

A messenger.A mind thatstays with you.

Kiko is a personal AI chat you deploy to your own Cloudflare account. You text one agent or a team of agents. They remember you, set reminders, use your connected apps, and keep working after you close the tab.

FEEL
Telegram-style chat, instant by design
HOSTING
Self-deployed on Cloudflare Workers and Durable Objects
PEOPLE
One owner per Worker, no sign-up
MODELS
Bring your own keys. Kiko sells none

01 / PROJECT AT A GLANCE

What Kiko is, and what makes it different

Kiko is for people who want one assistant for daily life and work, not a coding agent. This list is an editorial ranking by how distinctive each idea is as a product. It is not a score, and it is not a benchmark.

  1. Human pacing

    An agent waits for a pause, reads your whole burst of messages together, and answers once in short bubbles. It can also react with an emoji and say nothing.

    Section 04

  2. Work that survives a closed tab

    The agent stores your message in its own inbox, sets an alarm, and returns. Alarms do the work, so the run does not depend on your browser or on one request staying open.

    Section 02

  3. The human outranks every agent

    Your message goes first in the inbox and can interrupt work. A slow read or shell call is stopped. A write or an outside call runs to its end.

    Section 04

  4. Instant, optimistic client

    The app paints from IndexedDB first. Every action applies locally before the server answers, and a chat socket resumes by sequence number.

    Section 03

  5. One chat shape, from one agent to a team

    A chat with one agent is a room with one member. Teams add a lead, tagging with @name, a shared task board, and loop limits.

    Section 04

  6. Background workers

    A talker keeps answering while up to three workers run slow work inside the same agent. Workers are not room members.

    Section 04

  7. Memory you can edit

    A life log and a per-chat log, each with a tree of one-line summaries. You can pin up to 10 lines and redact any line for good. Agents cannot.

    Section 05

  8. Recipes as editable files

    18 built-in habits, such as a morning briefing or a decision council. Each is a RECIPE.md in your drive. One tap in Kiko adds one, then the agent asks setup questions in chat.

    Section 06

  9. Quiet follow-ups

    Open loops track goals and promises. A daily pass may offer one gentle nudge. Silence is the common case, and there are no push notifications.

    Section 05

  10. Your keys, your Cloudflare account

    Credentials are encrypted in your own Worker and never sent back to the browser. One owner, one deployment, no accounts for anyone else.

    Section 08

02 / DURABILITY LAB

Follow one message through a closed tab and an evicted object

This is a schematic step-through, not a live test of a backend and not a fault injector. It shows what the design stores and what it may lose at each point. Persistent items survive in SQLite storage. Volatile items live in memory and can vanish.

Persistent: stored in SQLite or IndexedDBVolatile: lives in memory onlyStruck through and marked (lost): gone after this step

What this does not promise. Alarms deliver at least once, and stable delivery ids let Kiko dedupe its own deliveries. That is not an exactly-once guarantee for arbitrary outside side effects, such as an email or a purchase made by a tool. A model stream may restart from saved state, so the new answer is complete but is not a byte-perfect continuation mid-token. Partial output is written in windows of about 2 seconds, so a crash can lose up to 2 seconds of text that was already going to be replaced. A message you typed while fully offline waits in the browser outbox and is sent when the app reconnects, not before.

Evidence in docs/architecture.md
  • Section 4: Durable Object layout, names, and 4.4 alarms (heartbeat at most 30 seconds ahead, idempotent handlers).
  • Section 7: agent team protocol. 7.3 receive() stores, sets an alarm, returns, and ignores ids it already has.
  • Section 9: realtime transport. 9.2 resume by seq, 9.3 optimistic patches stored in IndexedDB.
  • Section 17: scale and cost. 17.5 write coalescing and its crash test.

04 / HUMAN PACING AND TEAMS

Read the burst, answer once, yield to the human

The room waits for a pause. Default waits are constants in one file: 1 second after a message, 2.5 seconds after a fragment or while you are still typing, and no more than 8 seconds for one burst.

THREE SAMPLE MESSAGES

move the call to 3and invite danaactually make it 3:30
PAUSE, THEN ONE DELIVERY

ONE CONTEXT, ONE RUN

Moved the call to 3:30 and invited Dana. Shall I send her the agenda too?

When you write mid-run

Run stateAction
Nothing posted, no tool startedRestart with both turns
A read or shell tool is runningStop it, steer in your message
Bubbles posted, or a tool round finishedSteer after the round
You press stopAbort, submit nothing

A burst restarts a run at most twice. Writes, messages, and outside calls are not stopped midway.

Who is in the room

Lead. Gets untagged messages, splits goals into tasks, and may add agents.

Teammates. Members with their own bubbles. They are woken only when tagged or asked.

Background workers. Up to three child conversations inside one agent. They run slow work, report back, and are not room members.

Limits: 24 hops, 60 agent deliveries per human message, 8 members per room.

Task board with dependencies

A finished task wakes each task that waited on it, once. Try it.

05 / MEMORY, ROUTINES AND OPEN LOOPS

Memory is a log, and context is a view of it

Kiko keeps a life log for you and a log per room. A binary tree of one-line summaries sits over each, so a run starts with a bounded view that costs about the same at turn 50 or turn 50,000.

Tools recall and zoom open anything the view hides. A summary is one lowercase line of at most 280 bytes.

You hold the pen

The memory screen lists every line. You can pin up to 10 lines to head what agents read, and redact a line to remove its text for good. Agents can write notes but cannot pin or redact.

Routines

Ask in chat and an agent creates a routine. A trigger is a schedule (an RRULE no faster than every 30 minutes), a webhook (up to 64 KB, checked by token), a watch on a page, or a human message. A run with nothing to say answers [skip] and posts nothing.

Open loops

Goals, promises, and things you wait on are kept as loops. Most appear only as a quiet line in the next run's brief. About once a day, one overdue loop may become one gentle nudge, never more. After 7 days away, routines and follow-ups pause until you open Kiko.

No push notifications. Everything arrives as a message in a chat. That is a stated non-goal, so a reminder is a chat message you see when you look.

    07 / FILES, SKILLS, APPS, MODELS AND BROWSER

    The tools agents work with

    Agents use their tools without an approval step. Reading the rows below tells you what each tool can reach.

    Files every chat has a file space

    Each chat has one file space rooted at /. Your attachments land under attachments/, and agents write anywhere in the space. The Files tab shows a tree with editable text, rendered markdown, and pictures. Uploads start the moment a file is picked.

    Storage is R2 when a bucket is bound. Without one, files stay in Durable Object storage, up to 100 MB a file and 800 MB a chat or drive.

    Skills SKILL.md files agents load on demand

    A skill has a name and a description. Agents see the list and open a body only when a task needs it. Type /skill:name to apply one by hand. Built-in skills are for agents. Your own skills are recipes.

    Connected apps through Composio

    Paste one Composio project key in settings. Kiko offers whatever tools Composio lists at run time, with no tool names hardcoded. You connect apps in your Composio account. Agents call those tools without asking first, so connect only what you are happy for them to use. Disconnecting deletes the key at once.

    Models and keys bring your own

    Settings lists providers and models from the pi-ai catalog at run time. Kiko ships no model list and no provider adapter of its own. Connect with an API key or a provider sign-in flow. The app shows the last four characters of a key and nothing else. Pick a model per chat and per agent.

    Real browser optional, through Kernel

    With your own Kernel key, agents get one shared real browser for pages that need JavaScript, a login, or clicks. Only workers drive pages, so the talker keeps answering you. You can watch the live view or take over, and a takeover lasts at most 30 minutes. The live view opens in its own browser tab. There is no embedded browser panel in Kiko.

    Custom extensions parked, not shipped

    User extensions were parked on 2026-10-15. The code sits in core/extensions/ and nothing imports it, so none of it reaches the Worker. Do not expect extensions in a deployed Kiko.

    Self-deploy in brief

    1. Fork and clone, then bunx wrangler login.
    2. Optional: create the R2 bucket.
    3. bun install, build the client, deploy the Worker.
    4. Set SESSION_SECRET, CREDENTIALS_KEK, ACCESS_CODE.
    5. Open the URL, enter the code, add a model key.

    Data boundaries

    Your chats, memory, and files live in your Cloudflare account. Provider keys are encrypted in the owner's object and are never logged or sent to the browser.

    When a run calls a model or a connected app, that request reaches the provider you chose. Their terms apply to what you send them.

    Scope boundaries

    One owner per Worker. No sign-up, no second user, no billing, and no model service paid for by Kiko. The access code and the owner's session are the whole access model.

    Not built: a coding agent, containers, a terminal, share links, notifications.
